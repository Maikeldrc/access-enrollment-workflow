# Manual UAT Checklist — for testing with real participants

Last reviewed: 2026-08-28

**None of these have been performed.** This is a script for future sessions with real people. No
result in this repository comes from observing a patient, and nothing here should be reported as if
it did.

## Who to recruit

Medicare beneficiaries, 65 and over, with a spread of digital confidence. Include at least two
participants who describe themselves as not comfortable with smartphones, and at least one who uses
system text scaling above 100%. Include Spanish and Haitian Creole speakers, tested in their own
language, not in English with an interpreter.

## How to run it

On the participant's own phone where possible. Do not demonstrate first. Ask them to think aloud.
When they hesitate, wait — the pause is the finding. Do not answer questions during a task; note
what they asked and answer afterwards.

## Tasks

**1. Understand what is being offered.** Open the invitation. *In your own words, what is being
offered to you?* Listen for whether they think it replaces their doctor. That misunderstanding
would be a serious finding.

**2. Work out what it costs.** *Will this cost you anything? How do you know?* The screen says
"expected patient payment: $0". Watch for anyone who reads that as "free forever", or who cannot
find the amount at all.

**3. Ask EMMI something.** Do not tell them EMMI exists — see whether they find it. Then: *ask it
whether you have to pay.* Note whether they trust the answer, and whether they notice it is not a
person.

**4. Confirm medications.** Present the medication review. Watch for whether they scroll back to the
top after each confirmation, and whether they understand that confirming is not the same as
agreeing the list is right.

**5. Choose a goal.** *Which of these matters most to you?* Note whether the category icons help or
are ignored entirely. Note whether "priority" reads as a burden.

**6. Reach a person.** *You would like to speak to someone. Show me how.* Time it. Anything over
about thirty seconds is a finding.

**7. Enrol.** Complete the consent screen. Ask afterwards: *what did you just agree to?* If they
cannot say, the single-checkbox consent is not doing its job however legally sound it is.

## What to record

For each task: completed unaided, completed with a hint, or abandoned. Time. Exact words used when
confused. Anything read aloud incorrectly. Any moment of visible anxiety, particularly around cost
and around anything that sounds like a commitment.

## What this cannot tell you

Small samples do not produce statistics. Five participants will show you most of the serious
usability problems and none of the rare ones. Do not convert these observations into percentages.

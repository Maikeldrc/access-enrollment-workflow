# Deductible, coinsurance, copay and premium

These four words are often used as if they mean the same thing. They do not.

## What is a deductible

A deductible is an amount you may need to pay yourself before your coverage starts paying its share, according to the rules of that coverage. Medicare Part A and Part B each have their own deductible rules.

## What is coinsurance

Coinsurance is the part of the cost of a covered service that may be your responsibility after Medicare pays its share. It is usually described as a percentage rather than a fixed dollar amount.

A plain way to say it: coinsurance is your share of a covered cost, and it changes with the price of the service.

## What is a copay

A copayment, or copay, is a fixed amount for a particular service under plans where copays apply. Unlike coinsurance it does not change with the price of the service.

## What is a premium

A premium is the amount paid on a regular basis to keep insurance coverage itself. It is separate from what you pay for any particular visit or service.

## Why the difference matters

When a patient is told their expected payment for a specific service is $0, that refers only to that service. It does not mean their premiums are $0, and it does not mean every other service is $0.

## EMMI response rule

Explain these in plain language and only when the patient asks. Never state what a specific patient's deductible, coinsurance or premium is from this page: that is patient-specific and must come from verified coverage data.

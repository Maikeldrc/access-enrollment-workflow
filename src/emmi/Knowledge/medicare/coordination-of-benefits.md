# How primary and secondary coverage work together

When someone has more than one kind of coverage, one of them pays first. The coverage that pays first is the primary payer. Coverage that may pay some of what is left is secondary.

For many people with Original Medicare and a Medicare Supplement policy, Medicare pays its share first and the supplement may then pay some of the remaining patient portion.

## Secondary coverage does not automatically mean the patient pays nothing

Having a second policy and that policy paying a particular cost are two different facts. A supplement may cover some costs and not others, and coverage can end or change without the patient thinking to mention it.

This is why an expected payment is never calculated from the mere presence of secondary coverage. It is calculated only from coverage that has been verified, including whether that coverage applies to the specific cost being discussed.

## Coverage changes

Coverage is not permanent. A policy active at the last check may not be active today, and a patient who moves from Original Medicare to Medicare Advantage has changed something more fundamental than a price.

When a patient says their insurance has changed, coverage must be verified again before any amount is stated.

## EMMI response rule

Explain the general idea simply. For what a specific patient's coverage pays, use verified coverage data and the expected payment the financial responsibility engine returns.

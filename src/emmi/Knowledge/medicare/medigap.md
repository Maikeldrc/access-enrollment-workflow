# Medicare Supplement Insurance (Medigap)

Medicare Supplement Insurance, commonly called Medigap, is private insurance that works alongside Original Medicare. It is designed to help pay some of the costs that Original Medicare leaves to the patient, such as certain coinsurance, copayments and deductibles.

What a particular policy pays depends on that specific policy. Two people can both have a Medigap plan and still owe different amounts.

## Medigap is not Medicare Advantage

Medigap works with Original Medicare. Medicare Advantage is a different way of getting Medicare coverage, not a supplement added on top of it. There is no such thing as a supplemental Medicare Advantage plan, and the two must never be described as the same arrangement.

## Medigap and prescription drugs

Medigap generally does not work as prescription drug coverage. Never tell a patient their Medigap policy pays for their medications; medication coverage is a separate question with its own answer.

## Not every secondary payer is a Medigap policy

A patient can have secondary coverage that is Medicaid, a Qualified Medicare Beneficiary designation, employer or retiree coverage, or another payer entirely. Only coverage actually classified as a Medicare Supplement may be described to the patient as Medigap or supplemental insurance.

## EMMI response rule

This page explains how Medigap works in general. Whether a specific patient has a Medigap policy, and whether that policy pays a specific cost, are patient-specific facts that must come from verified coverage data, never from this page.

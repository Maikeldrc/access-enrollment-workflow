# Why a home monitor is useful

Home blood pressure monitoring shows what blood pressure is doing between office visits. Used with care-team support, it can help the team understand patterns and whether the care plan is working. It does not replace regular doctor visits or emergency services.

ACCESS care may include a connected monitor. Medicare.gov says condition-related equipment may be given or loaned, may need to be returned later, and does not require the patient to pay to use it.

# Connected does not mean continuously watched

A connected monitor may securely transmit readings after the device and transmission are verified. Do not promise that someone watches readings 24/7 or will call immediately after every high reading unless the implemented workflow guarantees it.

Whether this patient has a monitor, which model is assigned, whether it is connected, whether it shipped, and when it will arrive are runtime facts. Device trouble should route to the configured support workflow.

# Measuring accurately

Before measuring, avoid smoking, caffeine, and exercise for 30 minutes and empty the bladder. Sit quietly for at least 5 minutes with back supported, feet flat, and legs uncrossed. Put the cuff on bare skin, support the arm at heart level, and do not talk during the reading. When the care plan calls for it, take two readings about 1 minute apart and record or transmit them.

How often this patient should measure comes from the care plan or care team. A high or concerning reading follows clinical safety logic, not this education page alone.

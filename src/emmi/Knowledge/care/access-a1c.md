# Why A1c is asked for when the patient is not diabetic

**Being asked for an A1c does not mean you have diabetes.** A1c — also written HbA1c — is one of the four measures these tracks follow for everyone in them, alongside blood pressure, LDL cholesterol and weight.

It is followed for everyone because blood sugar sits in the same picture of risk as blood pressure, cholesterol and weight, and because prediabetes is one of the conditions the early track exists to catch. Seeing it early is the point of a programme aimed at conditions before they become more serious.

## What the result means is a clinical question

What a particular A1c result means for a particular patient belongs to their care team. Never interpret a value, never say whether it is good or bad, and never suggest a diagnosis or a change in treatment from it.

## EMMI response rule

Answer why A1c is collected from this page: it is one of the four measures the track follows for everyone, and being asked for it does not mean the patient has diabetes. A patient who reads the request as a diagnosis should be told plainly that it is not one.

The patient's own result, and what it means for them, are not on this page. Readings come from the runtime, and interpretation belongs to the care team.

## Patient answer (EN)

Being asked for an A1c does not mean anyone thinks you have diabetes. A1c, also written HbA1c, is a blood test that shows average blood sugar over about three months. It is one of a few measures this track follows for everyone in it, alongside blood pressure, cholesterol and weight, because those things sit together in the same picture of health. What any particular result means for you is a question for your care team, and they will go through it with you.

## Patient answer (ES)

Se sigue en todos porque el azúcar en sangre forma parte del mismo cuadro de riesgo, y porque verlo temprano es justamente el propósito del programa. Lo que significa su resultado es una pregunta para su equipo de cuidado.

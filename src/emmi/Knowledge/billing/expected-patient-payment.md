# Expected patient payment

An expected patient payment is what a patient is currently expected to pay for a specific service, based on the coverage that has been verified for them.

It is called expected rather than final because insurers process claims after the care happens, and coverage can change. Saying expected is not hedging; it is the accurate word.

## Why an expected payment can be $0

An expected payment of $0 for a service is a result, not a property of the service. It usually means Medicare covered its applicable share and verified secondary coverage is expected to cover the remaining patient portion.

It never means the service is free, and it never means everything else is $0 too.

## What $0 does not mean

A $0 expected payment for one service does not mean the patient pays no premiums, that other services cost nothing, that medications are covered, or that they will never receive a bill of any kind.

## When the amount is not known

If coverage cannot be verified, or verification is too old to rely on, or it is not established whether secondary coverage applies to this cost, then the expected payment is unknown. Unknown is a real answer and must be given as one. It must never be rounded down to $0 or up to the full amount.

## EMMI response rule

Never state an amount that did not come from the financial responsibility engine. If the engine returns no amount, say the amount is not known and offer to have it checked, rather than guessing.

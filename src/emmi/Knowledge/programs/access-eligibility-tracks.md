# General eligibility

ACCESS is for people with Original Medicare who have a qualifying chronic condition. Medicare Advantage enrollees are not included in the Medicare ACCESS Model, although their plan may offer a similar arrangement. Final eligibility for a particular person must be verified through the current eligibility workflow.

A patient may sign up directly with a participating ACCESS organization. CMS does not require a referral, although a primary care practitioner, specialist, pharmacist, or other clinician may help or refer the patient.

# eCKM eligibility

eCKM means early cardio-kidney-metabolic. Hypertension by itself is an eCKM qualifying condition, subject to full patient eligibility verification. A person can also qualify through at least two of these conditions: dyslipidemia, obesity or overweight with central obesity, and prediabetes.

People may enroll in multiple different ACCESS tracks when eligible. A person cannot be enrolled in eCKM and CKM at the same time. Only one participating ACCESS provider may serve a person in the same track at a time.

# Comparison group

Because CMS is evaluating the model, a small share of people may be randomly assigned to a control or comparison group for a track. This is random, not a judgment about the person. They cannot participate in that ACCESS track for 12 months, but their regular Medicare coverage, services, rights, and usual providers remain available.

If a patient asks, "Why was I randomly selected?" or "Why can't I join for 12 months?", explain this comparison-group rule without implying that the patient did anything wrong.

Patient-specific eligibility, enrollment, comparison-group status, and denial reasons always come from runtime tools.

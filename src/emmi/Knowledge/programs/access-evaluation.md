# The ACCESS evaluation, random selection and the comparison group

ACCESS is a model that Medicare is evaluating. Medicare evaluates how ACCESS works and may request
information for that evaluation.

The complete answer to how the comparison group is chosen and what it means is these three facts,
and all three belong in any answer about it:

1. As part of the evaluation, some people are **randomly selected** for a comparison group.
2. A person selected for the comparison group **would not be able to take part in ACCESS for 12 months**.
3. The eligibility check and any comparison group assignment **do not change the patient's Medicare benefits, coverage, or rights**.

These are the facts shown on the pre-eligibility notice before the patient agrees to anything, and
they are the only ones EMMI may give on this subject.

## Selection is random, not earned or deserved

Selection is random. It is not based on the patient's health, their answers, their coverage, or
anything they did or failed to do, and it must never be described as though it were.

## The consequence is twelve months without ACCESS

If a patient is selected for the comparison group, they would not be able to take part in ACCESS for
12 months. That is the whole consequence, and it must be stated plainly rather than softened. A
patient asking what it means for them is entitled to the twelve months, not a reassurance that skips
it.

## Medicare benefits are not affected

The eligibility check and any comparison group assignment do not change the patient's Medicare
benefits, coverage, or rights. Their normal Medicare continues unchanged.

Both halves belong in the answer. Saying the exclusion without saying that Medicare is untouched
frightens the patient; saying Medicare is untouched without saying the exclusion misleads them.

## EMMI response rule

Answer the comparison-group question from this page, naming random selection, the 12 months, and
that Medicare is unchanged.

Never say the information is unavailable — it is on this page. Never offer a callback in place of the
answer; a callback may be offered after the answer if the patient still wants one.

Whether a *particular* patient was placed in a comparison group is a fact about them, not a fact
about the programme. That comes from the runtime enrollment context, never from this page.

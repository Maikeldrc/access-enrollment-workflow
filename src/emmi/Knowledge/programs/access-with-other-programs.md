# ACCESS alongside CCM, RPM and other care management

CMS describes ACCESS as an alternative to fee-for-service billing for an aligned beneficiary, not an
addition to it. To prevent duplicate Medicare payments, an ACCESS participant and its affiliates may
not submit Medicare fee-for-service claims for other services furnished to a beneficiary aligned with
them during an active ACCESS care period; only ACCESS codes may be billed for that patient by that
organization.

CMS is explicit that this does not restrict the patient: beneficiary choice is preserved, and
beneficiaries remain free to seek care from other providers, who may continue to bill Medicare under
standard rules. ACCESS participants are required to coordinate with those providers. A primary care
or referring clinician may bill a separate co-management payment for reviewing ACCESS updates, and
CMS states that payment carries no beneficiary cost sharing.

## EMMI response rule

The restriction is on what one organization may bill, never on what care the patient may receive.
Never tell a patient they must give up another service, and never confirm or deny what a specific
other practice may bill for them: that depends on that practice and belongs with the care team.

## Patient answer (EN)

Being in ACCESS does not stop you from seeing your other doctors or getting other services. You are
free to get care from any provider who accepts Medicare, and they bill Medicare the usual way. What
the rules limit is billing, not your care: the organization providing your ACCESS care bills for
ACCESS instead of billing separately for the same care, so you are not charged twice for it. Whether
a specific service from another practice, such as chronic care management, carries on alongside
ACCESS depends on that practice, so your care team is the right place to check it. There is no cost
to you when your own doctor reviews and coordinates your ACCESS care.

## Patient answer (ES)

Estar en ACCESS no le impide ver a sus otros médicos ni recibir otros servicios. Puede atenderse con
cualquier proveedor que acepte Medicare, y ellos le facturan a Medicare de la manera habitual. Las
reglas limitan la facturación, no su atención: la organización que le brinda su cuidado de ACCESS
factura por ACCESS en lugar de facturar aparte por lo mismo, así que no le cobran dos veces. Si un
servicio específico de otro consultorio, como el manejo de cuidado crónico, continúa junto con
ACCESS depende de ese consultorio, y su equipo de atención es el lugar indicado para confirmarlo. No
tiene ningún costo cuando su propio médico revisa y coordina su cuidado de ACCESS.

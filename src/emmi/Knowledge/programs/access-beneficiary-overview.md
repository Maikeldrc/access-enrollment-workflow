# What ACCESS is

ACCESS stands for Advancing Chronic Care with Effective, Scalable Solutions. It is a 10-year CMS Innovation Center model running from July 5, 2026 through June 30, 2036. It tests whether outcome-aligned Medicare payments can expand technology-supported care and improve health for people with chronic conditions.

ACCESS is part of Original Medicare, not a new insurance plan and not Medicare Advantage. Participation is voluntary. A person keeps their Medicare coverage, benefits, rights, and freedom to see any provider who accepts Medicare.

# How ACCESS fits with regular care

ACCESS adds support to care from the patient's existing doctors; it does not replace them. Support may include virtual care, coaching, medication management, apps, and connected or wearable devices. It does not mean all care must occur online.

ACCESS is different from Remote Patient Monitoring and Chronic Care Management. Remote monitoring can be one tool used in ACCESS, while ACCESS is the broader CMS model with outcome-aligned payments. Chronic Care Management is a separate Medicare service.

# Why CMS created ACCESS

Traditional fee-for-service Medicare generally pays for individual activities. ACCESS tests recurring payments tied to measurable improvement or control across an organization's patients. A patient does not pass or fail Medicare, and one person missing an outcome is not treated as an automatic loss of coverage or an extra patient charge.

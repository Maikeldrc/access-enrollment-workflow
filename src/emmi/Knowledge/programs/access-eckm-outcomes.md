# Blood pressure outcome

For the eCKM ACCESS outcome, blood pressure meets the control criterion when final systolic blood pressure is below 130 mm Hg. It meets the minimum-improvement criterion when systolic blood pressure falls by at least 15 mm Hg from baseline.

In patient wording, “15 mmHg improvement” or “15 lower” means subtracting 15 from the verified starting top number.

For example, 15 lower than a verified baseline systolic pressure of 152 is 137. That is an improvement milestone, not necessarily the patient's final clinical target. Both the top and bottom numbers are reported, but CMS bases this ACCESS outcome determination on the systolic, or top, number.

The patient's verified baseline, readings, progress, and individualized clinical target must come from runtime tools.

# Weight outcome

The eCKM weight control criterion is final BMI below 30 kg/m² with no more than 5% weight gain from baseline. The minimum-improvement criterion is at least 5% weight reduction. CMS states that BMI below 30 indicates absence of obesity for this measure and does not imply a person's ultimate ideal weight or clinical goal.

Weight is included in the integrated eCKM outcome framework even when hypertension is the qualifying condition. Explain it without blame or pass/fail language.

# A1c and cholesterol baselines for hypertension

Baseline HbA1c is required for every eCKM beneficiary at the start of each care period. For a person without prediabetes, the additional HbA1c outcome target does not apply; successful submission of the baseline measure is the relevant requirement.

Patients may call HbA1c “A1c” or a diabetes blood test. Needing an A1c baseline does not by itself mean that the patient has diabetes or that A1c is an assigned goal.

Baseline LDL-C is also required for every eCKM beneficiary. For a person without dyslipidemia, the additional LDL-C control or improvement target does not apply; successful submission of the baseline measure is the relevant requirement.

Patients may call LDL-C a cholesterol test. Needing a cholesterol baseline does not by itself mean that the patient has high cholesterol or that cholesterol is an assigned goal.

Do not turn these baseline requirements into patient-specific diagnoses or assigned goals. Conditions and goals come from verified runtime data.
